Article 3: Disclosure Requirement (summary of core obligation).--
Where the claimed invention in a patent application is based on genetic resources, each Contracting Party shall require applicants to disclose the country of origin or, where the country of origin is not known, the source of the genetic resources. Where the claimed invention is based on traditional knowledge associated with genetic resources, each Contracting Party shall require applicants to disclose the Indigenous Peoples or local community, as applicable, who provided the traditional knowledge, or the source of the traditional knowledge, when that applicant knows, or has reasonable grounds to know, that the claimed invention is based on such traditional knowledge.

Practical read: this is the treaty that would, once in force, give international legal weight to a mandatory patent-disclosure requirement resembling India's own biological-material and geographical-origin disclosure rules (Patents Rules, biological-material-disclosure.md) and TKDL's defensive-disclosure purpose. Because it is not yet in force, the correct answer to "does GRATK require me to disclose my formulation's origin when filing abroad" is: not yet as binding international law, but (a) India's own Patents Rules already require this domestically regardless of GRATK's status, and (b) once GRATK enters into force, the same disclosure obligation would extend to other Contracting Parties' patent offices too. Never state GRATK as current binding law -- this is one of the two facts (along with WIPO GRATK's in-force status) the risk log explicitly calls out as something a judge could catch as a factual error.
