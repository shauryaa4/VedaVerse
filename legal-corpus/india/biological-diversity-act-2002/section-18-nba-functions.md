18. Functions and powers of National Biodiversity Authority.--
(1) The National Biodiversity Authority shall, with the approval of the Central Government, make regulations to provide for access to biological resources and traditional knowledge associated thereto, and for determination of fair and equitable sharing of benefits.
(2) It shall be the duty of the National Biodiversity Authority to regulate any activity referred to in sections 3, 4 and 6 by granting or rejecting approvals.
(3) The National Biodiversity Authority may--
(a) advise the Central Government on matters relating to the conservation of biodiversity, sustainable use of its components and fair and equitable sharing of benefits arising out of the utilisation of biological resources;
(b) advise the State Governments in the selection of areas of biodiversity importance to be notified under sub-section (1) of section 37 as biodiversity heritage sites, and measures for the management of such heritage sites;
(ba) advise the State Biodiversity Boards on any matter relating to the implementation of this Act;
(c) perform such other functions as may be necessary to carry out the provisions of this Act.
(4) The National Biodiversity Authority may, on behalf of the Central Government, take any measures necessary to oppose the grant of intellectual property rights in any country outside India on any biological resource found in or brought from India (including biological resources deposited in repositories outside India) or associated traditional knowledge accessed.

Practical read for the routing engine: sub-section (2) is the specific hook for the classification/ABS flow -- it is what makes the NBA the approving/rejecting authority for the access requests (section 3), research-transfer requests (section 4) and IP-filing approvals (section 6) that the ABS mock and escalation checklist should point users toward. Sub-section (4) is also relevant to the TKDL/prior-art angle: it is the NBA's statutory basis for opposing foreign patents on Indian biological resources or traditional knowledge -- useful context if a user asks "what happens if someone else patents this abroad."
